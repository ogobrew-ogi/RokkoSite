Trip 01

Full Day Tour of Sofia

From 110 EUR · Year-round · 7 hours

The capital, from Roman Serdica to today

Despite being young as a capital, Sofia carries centuries of layered history. This full-day tour threads together Roman ruins, a Cold War-era park and two of the city's best museums, with your guide connecting every layer along the way.

We start with a drive up Vitosha Mountain to the old chairlift station for a panoramic view over the city, then visit "The Bells" park, built in 1979 to commemorate peace. From there it's the National History Museum, the remains of Roman Serdica, St. Sofia Church, the Rotunda of St. George, and the golden-domed Alexander Nevsky Cathedral — the city's defining symbol.

A relaxed pace, ideal as a first day in the city or a standalone visit.

Trip 02

Valley of the Thracian Kings

From 140 EUR · Year-round · 10 hours

Tombs of a vanished royal dynasty

Long before Rome, this valley was the sacred burial ground of the Thracian kings — the same people behind the myth of Orpheus and the gladiator Spartacus. Known today as the Valley of the Roses, it holds roughly 1,300 burial mounds, of which some 300 have been excavated.

We visit five of the finest examples of Thracian funerary architecture, including the UNESCO-listed Kazanlak tomb with its remarkably preserved 4th-century BC frescoes, the three-chambered tomb of King Seuthes III at Golyama Kosmatka, and the uniquely decorated ceiling of the Ostrusha tomb — finishing at the History Museum in Kazanlak.

Trip 03

Plovdiv — History and Craft Beer

From 140 EUR · Year-round · 8+ hours

Bulgaria's oldest city, one cobblestone at a time

One of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Europe, Plovdiv rewards slow walking. Your guide leads you through the pastel Revival-era houses of the Old Town and its small museums, before the afternoon turns unhurried in Kapana — "The Trap" — the city's craft-beer and arts quarter.

To close the day, we visit Bulgaria's oldest brewery, founded in 1881, for a tour of its microbrewery housed in the original production halls, a tasting of its best beers, and a proper lunch on site.

Trip 04

Hiking in Pirin & Bansko

From 140 EUR · May–Oct · 11 hours

Alpine lakes beneath Vihren peak

Pirin is one of Bulgaria's UNESCO World Heritage natural parks, and this is the easiest way to see it in a single day. From Vihren hut, a moderate 3.5-hour round trip takes you past several of the glacial Banderishki lakes, with the mountain's highest peak, Vihren, visible throughout.

We descend to Bansko, Bulgaria's largest ski resort, for a traditional lunch and a short guided walk through its historic quarter, where 18th-century Revival-period architecture tells the town's story from before the ski lifts arrived.

Trip 05

Buzludzha Monument & Museum of Socialist Art

From 140 EUR · Year-round · 8+ hours

A flying saucer on a communist peak

Few buildings anywhere capture the ambition and collapse of a political era like Buzludzha. Built in 1981 by the Bulgarian Army to commemorate socialism, it stood abandoned after 1989 and has become one of the most photographed ruins in the Balkans.

We start in Sofia at the Museum of Socialist Art, a 7,500 sqm sculpture park with over 70 monumental works, including the five-pointed star that once crowned Sofia's former Party House. From there it's a scenic three-hour drive to Buzludzha itself, where the concrete saucer still crowns the mountain.
